An electrical inspection at home is getting urgent. Several electricians have come by. Each time,
they see the cabinet and walk away without looking back. Some promise to send a quote. No quote
has ever arrived.

So it is in my hands. I am going to learn.

![The electrical cabinet: rows of PHC modules, push-button cables and breakers, with wires everywhere](@/assets/images/phc-cabinet.jpg)

## What I am dealing with

The house runs on a PHC home automation system installed about twenty years ago, and the brand is
no longer maintained. Every bedroom has a panel of four or five push buttons, wired with thin
cables that look like Ethernet back to the cabinet – not a simple fuse board, a large cabinet
full of wires.

Then we built a kitchen at the back, which meant knocking down a wall. We removed the kitchen
cables ourselves, and an electrician came to connect the new ones. But there is no switch on that
side. For now, I made do with what was there: I linked the new LEDs to push buttons that already
controlled some lights on that side.

What the kitchen really needs is two more switches. Adding them would mean reprogramming almost
the whole house. And I have no plan of the installation – not on paper, not in a file.

## Keep the home automation, or rip it out?

I don't know yet which is better. I could replace the old system with a modern home automation,
or rewire the house with a simple system. Either way, cables have to be pulled, at least for the
switches, and it would cost a lot if someone else did the work. I need to analyse both options
before choosing.

## How I will learn

I know nothing about electricity yet. It is a field that interests me, but it is a whole
universe, and it will take time.

I will look for a good YouTube playlist or a site to learn the basics. And I want to see how far
AI can help with the installation itself: share photos of the real wiring, generate diagrams,
understand what is in place and how it could change.

## Like a developer

The house is like a legacy codebase nobody wants to maintain. So I will approach it the way I
would as a developer: analyse what exists and how it works, debug the situation, take an
inventory of the circuits, document everything, then write a plan for the changes.

## What's next

First, the basics of electricity – I won't touch anything without them. Then, mapping the
installation: which button drives which light, which cable goes where. I don't know yet how fast
this will go. I will write about it as I go.
