Our house needs an electrical inspection soon. Several electricians came to see it. Each time,
they looked at the cabinet and left. Some said they would send a quote. None of them did.

So it is up to me. I am going to learn.

![The electrical cabinet: rows of PHC modules, push-button cables and breakers, with wires everywhere](@/assets/images/phc-cabinet.jpg)

## What I have at home

The house has a home automation system from about twenty years ago. The brand is PHC, and nobody
maintains it anymore.

Every bedroom has a panel with four or five push buttons. Thin cables, a bit like Ethernet cables,
go from each panel to the cabinet. It is not a normal fuse box. It is a big cabinet full of wires.

Then we built a new kitchen at the back of the house. We knocked down a wall and removed the old
kitchen cables ourselves. An electrician came to connect the new ones. But there is no light
switch on that side of the room.

For now, I found a workaround. I connected the new LED lights to push buttons that already
existed.

The kitchen needs two more switches. But to add them, I would have to reprogram almost the whole
house. And I have no plan of the installation. Not on paper, not on a computer.

## Keep the home automation, or remove it?

I don't know yet. I see two options:

- replace the old system with a modern home automation;
- remove it and rewire the house with simple switches.

Both options mean new cables, at least for the switches. And both would cost a lot if I paid
someone to do it. I need to study both before I choose.

## How I will learn

I know nothing about electricity yet. It interests me, but it is a big field. It will take time.

I will start with a good YouTube playlist or a website for the basics. I also want to see how much
AI can help. I can share photos of the real wiring, ask it to draw diagrams, and use it to
understand what is there and what I could change.

## Like a developer

The house is like a legacy codebase nobody wants to maintain. So I will work on it like a developer:

1. understand what exists and how it works;
2. find what is broken;
3. list every circuit;
4. write everything down;
5. plan the changes.

## What's next

First, I learn the basics. I won't touch anything before that. Then I will map the
installation: which button turns on which light, and where each cable goes.

I don't know how fast it will go. I will write about it step by step.
